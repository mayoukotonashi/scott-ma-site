Science, Nature, and the Meiji Restoration

The people who took power in Japan in 1868 said they were restoring an ancient form of imperial rule. They also set about building institutions to teach modern science. How did those two ambitions come to make sense together?

I'm taking a new stab at an old question: Why did an imperial restoration movement end up destroying the traditions they claimed to restore? — from a history of science perspective: How did "natural science" come into being in the nineteenth century without a stable concept for "nature"?
